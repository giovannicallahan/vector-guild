# Probability pack (`prob`) — concept plan

Pack id `prob`, name "Probability", 40 learn missions (`q01`–`q40`) + 120 practice (`qNNb/c/d`) = 160. Format: `docs/PACK-FORMAT.md` (every rule there applies; read "found" as "prob"), in the shape of `docs/CALC1-PLAN.md`.

**Player:** an adult who has finished Foundations (arithmetic, fractions, decimals, percent, ratio, algebra, lines, functions, exponentials and logarithms, square roots, Σ for sums, data in a grid) and nothing more. No counting rules, sets or logic are assumed: `disc` owns counting, so this pack teaches only the little it needs (listing outcomes in a grid, and the number of orders of successes in q19). Every word and symbol below is taught in the mission that first uses it: outcome, sample space, event, probability, P(A), equally likely, relative frequency, complement A′, "A and B", "A or B", Venn diagram, mutually exclusive, conditional probability, P(A | B), multiplication rule, independent, tree diagram, Bayes' rule, odds, random variable, X, probability distribution, expected value E(X), μ, variance σ², standard deviation σ, trial, success, failure, p and q, binomial distribution, number of orders, geometric distribution, Poisson distribution, λ, k! (as notation only), continuous random variable, density curve, uniform distribution, exponential distribution, decay rate m, memoryless, normal distribution, standard normal Z, z-score, empirical rule, percentile, sample mean X̄, standard error σ/√n, law of large numbers, Central Limit Theorem, normal approximation.

**Setting:** the Kepler outpost's supply runs have started, and nothing is certain any more: shuttles arrive late, crates crack, seeds fail to sprout and the radio picks up meteor pings. Mira keeps an odds board in the dispatch hut and wants every guess turned into a number.

## Concepts (one learn mission each; section in brackets; ← prerequisites)

1. q01 Outcomes, sample space and equally likely probability [CM 7.5, IS 3.1] ← none
   Asked: P(colour) for a token drawn from a bag, numerator and denominator in lowest terms. Varies: counts of each colour.
2. q02 Probability as long-run relative frequency [IS 3.1] ← q01
   Asked: relative frequency of on-time runs in a log (decimal), then the expected number on time in the next N runs. Varies: log counts, N.
3. q03 The complement rule [CM 7.5] ← q01
   Asked: P(A′) = 1 − P(A) as a decimal, and as a count out of a stated total. Varies: P(A), total.
4. q04 Two-stage outcomes in a grid or tree [CM 7.4] ← q01
   Asked: number of cells in a grid of two spinners, then P(sum = s) as a fraction. Varies: spinner sizes, s.
5. q05 Events: "and", "or", Venn diagrams, mutually exclusive [IS 3.2, 3.5] ← q01
   Asked: counts in "A or B" and in "A and B" read from a Venn diagram of crate counts. Varies: region counts.
6. q06 The addition rule [IS 3.3, CM 7.8] ← q05
   Asked: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B) as a decimal. Varies: three probabilities.
7. q07 Conditional probability from a two-way table [IS 3.4] ← q06
   Asked: P(A | B) from a table of crates by supplier and condition, numerator and denominator. Varies: table cells.
8. q08 The multiplication rule: drawing without replacement [IS 3.3, CM 7.9] ← q07
   Asked: P(both faulty) for two crates drawn from a stack, numerator and denominator. Varies: stack size, faulty count.
9. q09 Independent events [IS 3.2] ← q08
   Asked: P(A) × P(B), then 1 if A and B are independent, 0 if not, comparing with the given P(A and B). Varies: probabilities.
10. q10 Tree diagrams and the total probability of an outcome [IS 3.5] ← q08
   Asked: overall P(damaged) across two or three suppliers, to 4 decimal places. Varies: shares and damage rates.
11. q11 Chapter review: tables, trees and rules mixed [IS 3.3, 3.4] ← q09, q10
   Asked: one conditional and one "or" probability from the same table. Varies: table cells.
12. q12 Bayes' rule: reversing a condition [PDS 3.4] ← q10
   Asked: P(ill | positive) for a medical-bay screen, to 3 decimal places. Varies: base rate, hit rate, false-alarm rate.
13. q13 Odds for and against [CM 7.7] ← q03
   Asked: odds against as two whole numbers a : b in lowest terms, or a probability from given odds. Varies: probability.
14. q14 Random variables and probability distributions [IS 4.1] ← q02
   Asked: the missing probability in a table (they sum to 1), then P(X ≥ k). Varies: table, k.
15. q15 Expected value E(X) = Σ x P(x) [IS 4.2, CM 7.11] ← q14
   Asked: expected number of repairs a day. Varies: table.
16. q16 Variance and standard deviation of a random variable [IS 4.2] ← q15
   Asked: σ² and σ to 2 decimal places. Varies: table.
17. q17 Chapter review: is the game worth playing? [CM 7.11, IS 4.2] ← q15, q12
   Asked: expected net gain per play, then the ticket price that makes E = 0. Varies: prizes, chances.
18. q18 Repeated independent trials: one particular sequence [IS 4.3] ← q09
   Asked: P(S, S, F, S, …) = pᵏqⁿ⁻ᵏ to 4 decimal places. Varies: n ≤ 8, p, the sequence.
19. q19 How many orders: where the successes can fall [IS 4.3, CM 7.10] ← q18, q04
   Asked: the number of orders of k sprouts in a row of n seeds (n ≤ 8), found by listing and by the triangle of orders built in the mission. Varies: n, k.
20. q20 The binomial probability P(X = k) [IS 4.3, CM 7.10] ← q19
   Asked: P(exactly k of n sprout) to 4 decimal places. Varies: n, k, p.
21. q21 Binomial mean np, spread √(npq), and "at least one" [IS 4.3] ← q20, q16
   Asked: np and √(npq) to 2 decimal places, or P(X ≥ 1) = 1 − qⁿ. Varies: n, p.
22. q22 Geometric distribution: waiting for the first success [IS 4.4] ← q18
   Asked: P(first sprout on seed k) = qᵏ⁻¹p to 4 decimal places, and the mean 1/p. Varies: p, k.
23. q23 Chapter review: binomial or geometric? [IS 4.3, 4.4] ← q21, q22
   Asked: 1 for binomial or 2 for geometric, then the probability asked. Varies: story, numbers.
24. q24 Poisson distribution: counts of rare events [IS 4.6] ← q20
   Asked: P(X = k) = λᵏe^(−λ) ÷ k! to 4 decimal places; k! = k × (k − 1) × … × 1 is taught here as notation. Varies: λ, k ≤ 5.
25. q25 Probability as area; the uniform distribution [IS 5.1, 5.2] ← q14
   Asked: P(c < X < d) for a wait spread evenly from a to b minutes. Varies: a, b, c, d.
26. q26 Uniform mean, percentiles and conditional chances [IS 5.2] ← q25, q07
   Asked: the kth percentile in minutes, or P(X > c | X > d). Varies: a, b, k, c, d.
27. q27 The exponential distribution [IS 5.3] ← q25
   Asked: P(part fails before x hours) = 1 − e^(−mx) to 4 decimal places, and the mean 1/m. Varies: m, x.
28. q28 Memoryless waiting [IS 5.3] ← q27, q07
   Asked: P(X > a + b | X > a) to 4 decimal places (it equals P(X > b)). Varies: m, a, b.
29. q29 Chapter review: the right model for a count or a wait [IS 4.6, 5.3] ← q24, q28
   Asked: model index (1 Poisson, 2 uniform, 3 exponential), then the probability. Varies: story, numbers.
30. q30 The normal curve and the empirical rule [IS 6.1] ← q16, q25
   Asked: percentage of bolts between two marks such as μ − σ and μ + 2σ (81.5). Varies: μ, σ, the marks.
31. q31 z-scores [IS 6.1] ← q30
   Asked: z = (x − μ) ÷ σ to 2 decimal places, or x for a given z. Varies: μ, σ, x.
32. q32 Area to the left from a normal table [IS 6.1, 6.2] ← q31
   Asked: P(X < x) to 4 decimal places; the prompt prints the table rows it needs. Varies: μ, σ, x (z drawn to 2 places).
33. q33 Probability between two values [IS 6.2] ← q32
   Asked: P(a < X < b) to 4 decimal places. Varies: μ, σ, a, b.
34. q34 Percentiles: from an area back to a value [IS 6.2] ← q32
   Asked: the length at the kth percentile to 1 decimal place, the z from a printed row. Varies: μ, σ, k.
35. q35 Sums of random variables: means add [IS 7.2] ← q15
   Asked: expected total load of n crates, nμ. Varies: n, μ.
36. q36 Spread of a sum: variances add when independent [IS 7.2] ← q35, q16
   Asked: standard deviation of the total, √n σ, to 2 decimal places. Varies: n, σ.
37. q37 The sample mean, standard error and the law of large numbers [IS 7.1, 7.3] ← q36
   Asked: σ ÷ √n, and the n that halves it. Varies: σ, n.
38. q38 Central Limit Theorem: probability for a sample mean [IS 7.1] ← q37, q32
   Asked: P(X̄ > c) for n daily readings, to 4 decimal places. Varies: μ, σ, n, c.
39. q39 Normal approximation to the binomial [IS 7.3] ← q21, q38
   Asked: z for k sprouts out of n using np and √(npq), then the approximate probability to 4 decimal places. Varies: n ≥ 30, p, k.
40. q40 Realm review and bridge to Data and inference: how surprising is this sample? [IS 7.3] ← q38, q39
   Asked: P(sample mean or proportion at least as far out as the one observed), to 4 decimal places. Varies: model and sample.

## Answers
Numbers only: fractions in two boxes (lowest terms) when the exact fraction is the point, otherwise decimals with the rounding stated and a tolerance sized to it. A yes/no question (q09) is typed as 1 or 0; a choice of model (q23, q29) is an index from a numbered list in the prompt. The game is offline, so a normal-table area is never looked up elsewhere: the prompt prints the two or three table rows it needs, and the generator draws z to two places from a stored table. Draw counts so fractions stay small and probabilities are not 0 or 1.
