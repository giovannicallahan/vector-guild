# Waves pack (`waves`) — concept plan

Pack id `waves`, name "Waves: sound and light", 40 learn + 120 practice = 160 missions (`q01`–`q40`, practice `qNNb/c/d`). Format: `docs/PACK-FORMAT.md` (every rule there applies; read "found" as "waves").

**Player:** an adult who has finished Foundations, Calculus 1 and Mechanics and nothing more: trigonometry in radians, derivatives and simple integrals, vectors, Newton's laws, springs and Hooke's law, kinetic and potential energy, power, circular motion, angular velocity ω. Electricity and magnetism are not assumed: light is treated as a wave with a known speed, and "electromagnetic" is only named. Words and symbols this pack teaches, each in the mission that first uses it: oscillation, period (T), frequency (f), hertz (Hz), simple harmonic motion, amplitude (A), angular frequency (ω), phase, phase shift (φ), equilibrium, pendulum, damping, resonance, natural frequency, wave, medium, transverse, longitudinal, wavelength (λ), wave speed, wave number (k), wave function y(x, t), linear mass density (μ), intensity (I), inverse-square law, superposition, constructive and destructive interference, standing wave, node, antinode, harmonic, fundamental, sound, decibel (dB), sound intensity level (β), open and closed pipe, beats, beat frequency, Doppler effect, Mach number, shock wave, light, ray, speed of light (c), index of refraction (n), law of reflection, Snell's law, critical angle, total internal reflection, polarisation, Malus's law, mirror, lens, focal length (f), image distance, magnification (m), double slit, fringe, order (m), path difference, small-angle approximation, thin film, interferometer, diffraction, single slit, grating, resolution, Rayleigh criterion, Bragg's law.

**Setting:** the Kepler outpost has started listening and looking. Mira runs the Signal Ridge: seismometers on springs, tuned cables on the comms mast, the echoing assembly hall, sirens on the rover track, and an observatory with lenses, gratings and a crystal lab.

**Sources:** OpenStax University Physics Volume 1 (chapters 15–17) and Volume 3 (chapters 1–4). URL patterns `https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/<section-slug>` and `https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/<section-slug>`; a source title reads `OpenStax University Physics Volume 1 — 16.1 Traveling Waves` or `OpenStax University Physics Volume 3 — 1.3 Refraction` (publisher word "OpenStax"). ## Answers
Numbers only; a yes-or-no question (is the ray trapped? is this spot loud?) is typed as 1 or 0 and an order m as a whole number, never as a word. **Every prompt names the unit of each answer box**, and the player types the number only. Wavelengths of light are asked in nanometres and slit sizes in micrometres or millimetres, so no answer needs a power of ten. Each rounded answer says how ("to 3 decimal places"), with tolerance as in `qa/pack-checks.js`; small angles in optics need 3–4 decimal places. Angles are in radians throughout, never degrees; q25 says so and teaches sin and arcsin of radians on the calculator. Constants in the prompt: g = 9.8 m/s², c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s, I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m², speed of sound 343 m/s unless the temperature is given. Signs carry meaning in q28 (a negative image distance is a virtual image).
