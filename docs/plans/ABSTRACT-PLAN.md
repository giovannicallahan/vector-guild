# Abstract algebra pack (`abstract`) — concept plan

Pack id `abstract`, name "Abstract algebra: patterns of moves", 40 learn + 120 practice = 160. Format: `docs/PACK-FORMAT.md` (every rule there applies; read "found" as "abstract"); files split by letter a–d and practice files 1–8 exactly as `docs/CALC1-PLAN.md`.

**Player:** an adult who has finished Foundations (arithmetic, fractions, algebra, functions, powers, polynomials) and Linear algebra (vectors, matrices, matrix products, determinants, rotations and reflections as matrices). Nothing from Discrete mathematics is assumed: remainders, factorials, gcd and lcm are taught where first used.
New words and symbols, each taught in the mission that first uses it: remainder, mod, modulus, Z_n, clock arithmetic, binary operation, operation (Cayley) table, closure, identity element, inverse, associative, group, abelian, gcd, coprime, unit, U(n), order of a group |G|, order of an element, power gⁿ, cyclic group, generator ⟨g⟩, repeated squaring, permutation, composition ∘, cycle notation, lcm, transposition, even, odd, sign, n!, symmetric group S_n, alternating group A_n, dihedral group D_n, rotation, reflection, subgroup, coset, index [G:H], Lagrange's theorem, Euler's φ, Fermat's little theorem, conjugate, normal subgroup, homomorphism, kernel, image, isomorphism ≅, direct product ×, quotient group G/H, first isomorphism theorem, group action, orbit, stabiliser, orbit–stabiliser theorem, conjugacy class, Burnside's theorem, partition of a number, ring, zero divisor, field, Z_p, polynomial ring, root, factor theorem, ideal, irreducible, finite field GF(pᵏ).

**Setting:** the Kepler outpost's Vault Works, where Mira's crew builds combination dials, rotating airlock hatches, tiled dome floors, beacons that pulse on a beat and coded vault doors; in every one only the pattern of moves matters, not what the parts are made of.

## Concepts (one learn mission each; Judson section in brackets; "after" = prerequisites)

1. q01 Adding on a clock: Z_n [3.1] — after none — Asked: (a + b) mod n and (a − b) mod n; varies n 5–24, a, b up to 60.
2. q02 Multiplying on a clock [3.1] — after q01 — Asked: (a × b) mod n and a² mod n; varies n, a, b.
3. q03 Binary operations and operation tables [3.1, 3.2] — after q01 — Asked: two table entries of x ∗ y = (x + y + k) mod n; varies k, n, the row and column.
4. q04 Identity and inverses [3.2] — after q03 — Asked: the identity of x ∗ y = (x + y + k) mod n and the inverse of a; varies k, n, a.
5. q05 The group rules [3.2] — after q02, q04 — Asked: 1 or 0 for closure, identity and inverses of {1, …, n − 1} under × mod n (all 1 only when n is prime); varies n 4–20.
6. q06 Units and U(n) [3.2, 2.2] — after q05 — Asked: |U(n)| and the inverse of a in U(n); varies n ≤ 30, a coprime to n.
7. q07 Order of an element in Z_n [4.1] — after q01, q06 — Asked: the order of a, which is n ÷ gcd(a, n); varies n 6–40, a.
8. q08 Order of an element in U(n) [4.1] — after q06, q07 — Asked: the smallest k with aᵏ mod n = 1, from a table of powers; varies n ≤ 25, a.
9. q09 Cyclic groups and generators [4.1] — after q07 — Asked: the number of generators of Z_n, and 1 or 0 for "a generates Z_n"; varies n, a.
10. q10 Large powers through the order [4.1, 4.3] — after q08 — Asked: aᵏ mod n for k up to 1000 by reducing k mod the order, or by repeated squaring; varies a, k, n.
11. q11 Chapter review: orders and inverses mixed [4.1, 3.2] — after q09, q10 — Asked: an order and an inverse in one setting; varies all.
12. q12 Permutations and composition [5.1] — after q04 — Asked: (σ ∘ τ)(i) for two given i, σ and τ on 5–6 symbols; varies both permutations.
13. q13 Cycle notation and the order of a permutation [5.1] — after q12, q07 — Asked: the number of cycles and the order (lcm of cycle lengths); varies the permutation.
14. q14 Transpositions, even and odd [5.1] — after q13 — Asked: the transpositions needed (n minus the number of cycles) and the sign as 1 or −1; varies.
15. q15 Counting S_n and A_n [5.1] — after q14 — Asked: |S_n|, |A_n| and the number of transpositions n(n − 1) ÷ 2; varies n 3–7.
16. q16 Dihedral groups: turning and flipping a polygon [5.2] — after q12 — Asked: |D_n| and the vertex label where vertex v lands after a turn of r steps then a flip (−(v + r) mod n); varies n, v, r.
17. q17 Chapter review: symmetry moves mixed, with determinants ±1 of the matching matrices [5.2, 5.1] — after q15, q16 — Asked: an order, a sign and a vertex label; varies all.
18. q18 Subgroups of a cyclic group [3.3, 4.1] — after q09 — Asked: the number of subgroups of Z_n (its divisors) and |⟨a⟩|; varies n ≤ 60, a.
19. q19 Cosets [6.1] — after q18 — Asked: the number of cosets of ⟨d⟩ in Z_n (d divides n) and the smallest element of the coset of a; varies n, d, a.
20. q20 Lagrange's theorem [6.2] — after q19 — Asked: the index |G| ÷ |H|, and 1 or 0 for "a subgroup of size m can exist"; varies |G|, |H|, m.
21. q21 Euler's and Fermat's theorems [6.3] — after q20, q10 — Asked: aᵏ mod n using a^φ(n) mod n = 1; varies a, k up to 10000, n ≤ 50.
22. q22 Conjugates and normal subgroups [10.1] — after q12, q19 — Asked: (τ σ τ⁻¹)(i), and 1 or 0 for "H is normal" (A_n yes, ⟨(1 2)⟩ in S_3 no); varies σ, τ, i, the example.
23. q23 Chapter review: subgroups, cosets and Lagrange [6.1, 6.2] — after q20, q22 — Asked: a subgroup size, an index and a coset representative; varies all.
24. q24 Homomorphisms [11.1] — after q18 — Asked: φ(a) for φ(x) = kx mod n from Z_m to Z_n, and 1 or 0 for "φ is well defined" (n divides km); varies m, n, k, a.
25. q25 Kernel and image [11.1] — after q24 — Asked: |kernel| = gcd(k, n) and |image| = n ÷ gcd(k, n) for x ↦ kx on Z_n; varies k, n.
26. q26 Isomorphisms [9.1, 9.2] — after q25 — Asked: 1 or 0 for "Z_m × Z_n ≅ Z_mn" (gcd(m, n) = 1) and the largest element order lcm(m, n); varies m, n.
27. q27 Direct products [9.2] — after q26 — Asked: the order of (a, b) in Z_m × Z_n, and the x in Z_mn that maps to (a, b) when m, n are coprime; varies m, n, a, b.
28. q28 Quotient groups [10.1] — after q19, q25 — Asked: |Z_n / ⟨d⟩| and the order of the coset a + ⟨d⟩, which is d ÷ gcd(a, d); varies n, d, a.
29. q29 Chapter review: the first isomorphism theorem [11.2] — after q27, q28 — Asked: |image| = |G| ÷ |kernel| and one order in a product; varies all.
30. q30 Group actions and orbits [14.1] — after q16 — Asked: the orbit size of a bead pattern under the n turns of a ring (n ÷ its period); varies n, the pattern.
31. q31 Orbit–stabiliser theorem [14.1] — after q30, q20 — Asked: |stabiliser| = |G| ÷ |orbit| for D_n on vertices or the 24 cube turns on faces, edges or corners; varies the object.
32. q32 Conjugacy classes in S_n [14.2] — after q13, q31 — Asked: the number of permutations of a given cycle type, n! ÷ ∏ kᵐ m!; varies n 4–7, the type.
33. q33 Burnside's counting theorem [14.3] — after q31 — Asked: the colourings fixed by a turn of r steps (k^gcd(r, n)) and the number of different necklaces; varies n 3–8 beads, k 2–4 colours.
34. q34 Finite abelian groups [13.1] — after q27 — Asked: the number of abelian groups of order n (product of the partition counts of the prime exponents); varies n ≤ 500.
35. q35 Rings and zero divisors [16.3] — after q06 — Asked: the numbers of units and of zero divisors in Z_n (n − 1 − φ(n)); varies n ≤ 40.
36. q36 Fields: dividing in Z_p [16.4] — after q35 — Asked: x with (ax + b) mod p = c; varies prime p ≤ 31, a, b, c.
37. q37 Polynomials over Z_p [17.1, 17.2] — after q36 — Asked: the remainder of f(x) ÷ (x − a) in Z_p[x] (= f(a) mod p) and the number of roots of f in Z_p; varies p, f of degree 2–3, a.
38. q38 Ideals in the integers [16.5] — after q35 — Asked: the positive generator of the ideal made by a and b (their gcd), and 1 or 0 for "c is in it"; varies a, b, c.
39. q39 Irreducible polynomials [17.3] — after q37 — Asked: 1 or 0 for "x² + bx + c is irreducible over Z_p" (no root), and the count (p² − p) ÷ 2 of monic irreducible quadratics; varies p, b, c.
40. q40 Finite fields; realm review and bridge to Number theory [22.1] — after q39, q09 — Asked: |GF(pᵏ)|, the order pᵏ − 1 of its multiplication group and its number of generators φ(pᵏ − 1); varies p, k.

## Answers
Numbers only, never a set, a table or a proof. Structure becomes a number in five ways: an element of Z_n (typed as its least residue 0 … n − 1), a size or count (|G|, an index, an orbit, a number of necklaces), an order, a sign typed 1 or −1, and a property typed 1 (true) or 0 (false). A permutation is never typed: ask where one symbol goes. Every generator draws n, the elements and the permutations afresh; draw primes and divisors from fixed lists so answers stay small, and keep moduli under 50 unless the point is a large power.
