# Data and inference pack (`stats`) — concept plan

Pack id `stats`, name "Data and inference", 40 learn missions (`q01`–`q40`) + 120 practice (`qNNb/c/d`) = 160. Format: `docs/PACK-FORMAT.md` (every rule there applies; read "found" as "stats"), in the shape of `docs/CALC1-PLAN.md`.

**Player:** an adult who has finished Foundations and Probability, and nothing more. From Probability they know outcomes, events, the addition and multiplication rules, conditional probability, independence, random variables, expected value μ, variance σ² and standard deviation σ of a distribution, the binomial, Poisson, uniform and exponential distributions, the normal curve, z-scores, reading a normal table, the standard error σ/√n and the Central Limit Theorem. They have never handled a data set, an estimate or a test. Every word and symbol below is taught in the mission that first uses it: data, variable, population, sample, parameter, statistic, frequency, relative frequency, cumulative relative frequency, sample mean x̄, median, mode, weighted mean, percentile, quartile, Q1, Q3, interquartile range (IQR), five-number summary, box plot, outlier, fence, skewed, sample variance s², sample standard deviation s, n − 1, simple random, systematic and stratified samples, point estimate, confidence interval, confidence level, margin of error, critical value z*, Student's t distribution, degrees of freedom (df), t*, sample proportion p̂, null hypothesis H₀, alternative hypothesis Hₐ, left-, right- and two-tailed, significance level α, Type I and Type II errors, β, power, test statistic, p-value, reject, pooled proportion, paired differences, chi-square χ², observed and expected counts, goodness of fit, test of independence, scatter plot, correlation coefficient r, least-squares line ŷ = a + bx, slope b, intercept a, residual, r², extrapolation, ANOVA, sum of squares, mean square, F ratio.

**Setting:** the Kepler outpost now keeps records: farms, mines and the clinic file readings every day at the Records Office. Mira wants every outpost decision, from which seed to plant to whether a supplier is honest, backed by data rather than by hunches.

## Concepts (one learn mission each; section in brackets; ← prerequisites)

1. q01 Data, samples and frequency tables [1.3] ← none
   Asked: relative and cumulative relative frequency of one class of a log, as decimals. Varies: class counts.
2. q02 The mean of a data set, x̄ = Σx ÷ n [2.5] ← q01
   Asked: mean yield of 6–10 plots. Varies: readings.
3. q03 Median and mode [2.5] ← q02
   Asked: the median (odd or even n) and the mode. Varies: readings, n.
4. q04 The mean from a frequency table [2.5] ← q02
   Asked: weighted mean of crew sizes from a table. Varies: values, frequencies.
5. q05 Percentiles and quartiles [2.3] ← q03
   Asked: Q1 and Q3 by the book's median-of-halves method. Varies: 8–12 readings.
6. q06 IQR, box plots and outliers [2.3, 2.4] ← q05
   Asked: the upper fence Q3 + 1.5 × IQR, then the number of outliers. Varies: readings.
7. q07 Sample variance and standard deviation, dividing by n − 1 [2.7] ← q02
   Asked: s² and s to 2 decimal places. Varies: 5–7 readings.
8. q08 Skew and the z-score of a reading [2.6, 2.7] ← q07, q03
   Asked: mean minus median (its sign shows the skew), then z of one reading to 2 decimal places. Varies: data.
9. q09 Fair samples: simple random, systematic, stratified [1.2] ← q01
   Asked: crew members to take from each stratum for a proportional sample, or the label of the third unit in a systematic sample. Varies: stratum sizes, start, step.
10. q10 Sampling variability and the standard error of x̄ [7.1] ← q07
   Asked: σ ÷ √n to 2 decimal places, and the sample size that halves it. Varies: σ, n.
11. q11 Chapter review: summarising a survey [2.7, 7.1] ← q08, q10
   Asked: x̄, s and the standard error from one small survey. Varies: data.
12. q12 Point estimate, confidence level and margin of error (σ known) [8.1] ← q10
   Asked: margin of error z*σ ÷ √n to 2 decimal places, with z* = 1.645, 1.96 or 2.576 taught in the mission. Varies: σ, n, level.
13. q13 The confidence interval for a mean, and the sample size for a target margin [8.1] ← q12
   Asked: the lower and upper bounds, or the smallest n (rounded up). Varies: x̄, σ, n, margin.
14. q14 Student's t distribution and degrees of freedom [8.2] ← q12
   Asked: df = n − 1, then t* read from the printed table row. Varies: n, level.
15. q15 The t interval for a mean [8.2] ← q14, q07
   Asked: the margin and both bounds to 2 decimal places. Varies: data summary.
16. q16 The confidence interval for a proportion [8.3] ← q12
   Asked: p̂, the margin z*√(p̂(1 − p̂) ÷ n) to 3 decimal places, or the sample size needed. Varies: count, n, level.
17. q17 Chapter review: which interval? [8.1, 8.2, 8.3] ← q15, q16
   Asked: interval index (1 z, 2 t, 3 proportion), then its bounds. Varies: story, numbers.
18. q18 Null and alternative hypotheses [9.1] ← q13
   Asked: the value in H₀, then the tail as 1 left, 2 right, 3 two-tailed. Varies: claim wording, value.
19. q19 Type I and Type II errors, α, β and power [9.2] ← q18
   Asked: power 1 − β, or the expected number of false alarms in N tests at level α. Varies: β, α, N.
20. q20 The test statistic for a mean [9.5] ← q18, q12
   Asked: z = (x̄ − μ₀) ÷ (σ ÷ √n) to 2 decimal places. Varies: x̄, μ₀, σ, n.
21. q21 The p-value and the decision [9.4] ← q20
   Asked: the p-value to 4 decimal places from printed table rows, then 1 to reject H₀ or 0 not to. Varies: z, tail, α.
22. q22 Testing a proportion [9.5] ← q21, q16
   Asked: the z statistic to 2 decimal places, then the decision 1 or 0. Varies: p₀, count, n.
23. q23 Chapter review: a full test of a supplier's claim [9.4, 9.5] ← q19, q22
   Asked: the tail code, the statistic, the decision. Varies: claim, sample.
24. q24 Two independent means: the two-sample t statistic [10.1] ← q21, q15
   Asked: t to 2 decimal places (df printed in the prompt), then the decision. Varies: two summaries.
25. q25 Two proportions: the pooled proportion and z [10.3] ← q22
   Asked: the pooled proportion to 3 decimal places, then z to 2 decimal places. Varies: counts, sizes.
26. q26 Matched pairs: testing the differences [10.4] ← q24
   Asked: the mean difference, then t to 2 decimal places. Varies: 5–8 before-and-after pairs.
27. q27 Chi-square goodness of fit [11.2] ← q21
   Asked: the expected counts, then χ² = Σ(O − E)² ÷ E to 2 decimal places. Varies: observed counts, claimed shares.
28. q28 Chi-square test of independence [11.3] ← q27
   Asked: one expected count (row total × column total ÷ grand total), df, and χ². Varies: a 2 × 3 table.
29. q29 Chapter review: choosing a two-group test [10.1–11.3] ← q26, q28
   Asked: test index from a numbered list, then its statistic. Varies: story, data.
30. q30 Scatter plots and the correlation coefficient r [12.2, 12.3] ← q07
   Asked: r to 2 decimal places for 5 points, sums tabulated in the guided steps. Varies: points.
31. q31 The least-squares line: slope and intercept [12.3] ← q30
   Asked: b and a to 2 decimal places. Varies: points.
32. q32 Prediction, residuals and extrapolation [12.3, 12.5] ← q31
   Asked: ŷ at a given x, the residual y − ŷ, and 1 if x lies inside the data range or 0 if not. Varies: line, x, y.
33. q33 r²: the share of variation explained [12.3] ← q30
   Asked: r² as a percentage to 1 decimal place. Varies: r or points.
34. q34 Testing r, and outliers from a line [12.4, 12.6] ← q33, q32
   Asked: df = n − 2 and 1 if r beats the printed critical value, or the count of points more than 2s from the line. Varies: r, n, residuals.
35. q35 One-way ANOVA: variation between and within groups [13.1, 13.2] ← q24
   Asked: the sum of squares between groups and within groups. Varies: three small groups.
36. q36 The F ratio and the decision [13.2] ← q35
   Asked: both df, F to 2 decimal places, then 1 to reject or 0 against a printed critical value. Varies: groups.
37. q37 Testing a single variance [11.6] ← q27, q07
   Asked: χ² = (n − 1)s² ÷ σ² to 2 decimal places, then the decision. Varies: n, s, σ.
38. q38 Intervals and tests agree [9.4, 8.1] ← q13, q21
   Asked: 1 if a two-tailed test at α rejects μ₀ (μ₀ outside the interval) or 0, for three values of μ₀. Varies: interval, μ₀ values.
39. q39 Choosing the right test [9.5] ← q29, q36
   Asked: the index of the right test from a list of nine, then its df. Varies: story.
40. q40 Realm review and bridge: a full study from sample to conclusion [9.5] ← q38, q39
   Asked: the statistic, the p-value to 4 decimal places and the decision for one outpost study. Varies: study and data.

## Answers
Numbers only, with the rounding stated and a tolerance sized to it. Decisions are 1 (reject H₀) or 0; a tail is a code 1, 2, 3; a choice of interval or test is an index from a numbered list printed in the prompt. The game is offline, so z, t, χ², F and r critical values or areas are printed in the prompt as the few table rows needed, and generators draw only values that those stored rows cover. Data sets are drawn small (5–12 values) with tidy means, so the arithmetic stays by hand.
