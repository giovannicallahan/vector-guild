# Calculus 3 pack (`calc3`) — concept plan

Pack id `calc3`, name "Calculus 3: many variables", 40 learn missions (`q01`–`q40`) + 120 practice (`qNNb/c/d`) = 160. Format: `docs/PACK-FORMAT.md` (every rule there applies; read "found" as "calc3", and §11 as written for calc1).

**Player:** an adult who has finished Foundations, Calculus 1 and Calculus 2: limits, derivatives, integrals and their techniques, areas and volumes by slicing, arc length, work, centre of mass, improper integrals, series and Taylor polynomials, and a first look at parametric curves. Linear algebra is not assumed: vectors are taught again from the start, and no matrix is needed beyond writing a 2 × 2 or 3 × 3 determinant as a recipe where it is taught. Radians and SI units throughout. New words and symbols, each taught in the mission that first uses it: three-dimensional coordinates (x, y, z), vector, component, ⟨a, b, c⟩, i, j, k, magnitude ‖v‖, unit vector, scalar, dot product u · v, angle between vectors, orthogonal, projection, cross product u × v, torque, normal vector, parametric line, plane, vector-valued function r(t), velocity, speed, acceleration vector, function of two variables f(x, y), domain, level curve, contour map, limit along a path, partial derivative ∂f/∂x and fₓ, second partial derivatives, mixed partials, tangent plane, linear approximation, multivariable chain rule, directional derivative, gradient ∇f, steepest ascent, critical point, saddle point, discriminant D, Lagrange multiplier λ, constraint, double integral ∬, iterated integral, Fubini's theorem, type I and type II regions, polar coordinates (r, θ) and the factor r, triple integral ∭, cylindrical and spherical coordinates (ρ, φ), change of variables, Jacobian, vector field, line integral ∫_C, work along a path, conservative field, potential function, closed curve ∮, Green's theorem, divergence div F, curl, parametrised surface, surface area element, flux, orientation, Stokes' theorem, divergence theorem.

**Setting:** the Kepler outpost now works in three dimensions: a survey deck maps the crater, an orbital dock lines up approach paths, a weather mast reads wind and temperature fields across the plain, and an atmosphere plant pushes air through the domes. Mira is still the mentor. Objects are survey beacons, docking ports, solar panels, terrain maps, wind vanes, dome shells, cables and air ducts.

## Concepts (one learn mission each; OpenStax Calculus Volume 3 section in brackets; prerequisites after the arrow)

1. q01 Points in space and the distance between them [2.2] ← [] — Asked: the distance in metres. Varies: coordinates drawn to give whole distances.
2. q02 Vectors: components, adding and scaling [2.1] ← q01 — Asked: the three components of a combination such as 2u − v. Varies: u, v, scalars.
3. q03 Magnitude and unit vectors [2.2] ← q02 — Asked: the magnitude, then the components of the unit vector as fractions. Varies: whole-length vectors.
4. q04 Dot product and the angle between vectors [2.3] ← q03 — Asked: u · v, then the angle in radians to 3 decimal places. Varies: u, v.
5. q05 Projection; work as a dot product [2.3] ← q04 — Asked: the scalar projection, then the work in joules. Varies: force, displacement.
6. q06 Cross product; area of a parallelogram; torque [2.4] ← q04 — Asked: the components of u × v, then the area. Varies: u, v.
7. q07 Lines in space [2.5] ← q02 — Asked: the point at a given t, or the t where the line meets a level z. Varies: point, direction.
8. q08 Planes and their normal vectors [2.5] ← q06 — Asked: d in ax + by + cz = d through three points. Varies: points.
9. q09 Distance from a point to a plane [2.5] ← q08, q05 — Asked: the distance to 2 decimal places. Varies: plane, point.
10. q10 Vector-valued functions: velocity, speed, acceleration [3.2, 3.4] ← q03 — Asked: velocity components at t, then the speed. Varies: polynomial and trigonometric components.
11. q11 Chapter review: docking a shuttle [2.5, 3.4] ← q09, q10 — Asked: a meeting time and a speed. Varies: as q07 and q10.
12. q12 Functions of two variables and level curves [4.1] ← q01 — Asked: f(a, b), then the level c of the curve through a point. Varies: f, point.
13. q13 Limits along paths [4.2] ← q12 — Asked: the limit along y = mx for two slopes, then 1 if the limit exists, 0 if not. Varies: rational f, slopes.
14. q14 Partial derivatives [4.3] ← q12 — Asked: fₓ and f_y at a point. Varies: polynomial and exponential f.
15. q15 Second and mixed partial derivatives [4.3] ← q14 — Asked: fₓₓ, f_yy and fₓy at a point. Varies: f, point.
16. q16 Tangent planes and linear approximation [4.4] ← q14, q08 — Asked: the plane's two slopes, then the estimated value nearby. Varies: f, point, step.
17. q17 Chapter review: reading a terrain map [4.1, 4.3] ← q15, q16 — Asked: a height, a slope, an estimate. Varies: as above.
18. q18 The chain rule for several variables [4.5] ← q14 — Asked: dz/dt at a time. Varies: f and the path x(t), y(t).
19. q19 Directional derivatives and the gradient [4.6] ← q18, q04 — Asked: ∇f components at a point, then the rate in a given direction. Varies: f, point, direction.
20. q20 Steepest ascent [4.6] ← q19 — Asked: the greatest rate of increase (the length of ∇f), then the angle of its direction in radians. Varies: f, point.
21. q21 Critical points and the second derivative test [4.7] ← q15 — Asked: the critical point, D, and a code: 1 maximum, 2 minimum, 3 saddle. Varies: quadratic f.
22. q22 Lagrange multipliers [4.8] ← q20 — Asked: the largest value of f on the constraint, then λ. Varies: f, constraint.
23. q23 Chapter review: best values on the plain [4.6, 4.7] ← q21, q22 — Asked: a code and a value. Varies: as above.
24. q24 Double integrals over rectangles [5.1] ← q14 — Asked: the iterated integral. Varies: f, rectangle.
25. q25 Double integrals over general regions; switching the order [5.2] ← q24 — Asked: the value, then a new limit after switching. Varies: bounding curves.
26. q26 Double integrals in polar coordinates [5.3] ← q25 — Asked: k in k·π. Varies: radii, angles, integrand.
27. q27 Triple integrals [5.4] ← q25 — Asked: the volume or mass of a box or wedge. Varies: limits, density.
28. q28 Cylindrical and spherical coordinates [5.5] ← q26, q27 — Asked: k in k·π for a cylinder, cone or ball piece. Varies: radii, heights.
29. q29 Chapter review: the mass of a dome [5.1, 5.2, 5.5] ← q28 — Asked: a volume and a mass. Varies: radius, density.
30. q30 Change of variables and the Jacobian [5.7] ← q28 — Asked: the Jacobian, then the integral's value. Varies: linear maps u = ax + by.
31. q31 Vector fields; line integrals of a function [6.1, 6.2] ← q10 — Asked: F at a point, then the mass of a cable of given density. Varies: field, path.
32. q32 Work along a path: line integrals of a field [6.2] ← q31, q05 — Asked: the work in joules. Varies: field, straight or curved path.
33. q33 Conservative fields and potential functions [6.3] ← q32, q19 — Asked: 1 if conservative, 0 if not, then the work from the potential. Varies: field, endpoints.
34. q34 Green's theorem [6.4] ← q33, q25 — Asked: the circulation round a closed curve. Varies: field, rectangle or disc.
35. q35 Divergence and curl [6.5] ← q31 — Asked: div F and the components of curl F at a point. Varies: polynomial field.
36. q36 Parametrised surfaces and surface area [6.6] ← q06, q24 — Asked: the area, as k·π where it applies. Varies: plane piece, cylinder, sphere cap.
37. q37 Flux through a surface [6.6] ← q36 — Asked: the flux. Varies: field, surface, orientation.
38. q38 Stokes' theorem [6.7] ← q35, q37 — Asked: the circulation round the rim, through the curl. Varies: field, disc radius.
39. q39 The divergence theorem [6.8] ← q35, q37 — Asked: the flux out of a closed box or ball. Varies: field, size.
40. q40 Realm review and bridge to Differential equations [4.6, 6.7, 6.8] ← q38, q39 — Asked: a gradient rate, a flux, and the rate at which air leaves a dome. Varies: as above.

## Answers

Numbers only; a vector is typed as its components in separate boxes, never as a formula. A surface, field or region is never an answer: ask for a value at a point, a component, a rate, a k in k·π. Classifications are codes the mission lists (1 maximum, 2 minimum, 3 saddle; 1 or 0 for conservative, for "the limit exists"), always alongside a number. Generators draw coordinates so lengths, critical points and limits of integration are whole numbers or simple fractions.
